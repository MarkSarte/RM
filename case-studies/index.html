Revenue decisions measured by what happens next.

A pricing change is only an idea until the booking data proves whether it worked. Here are three examples of how different commercial problems required different revenue strategies.

Case study 01

Protecting peak demand without sacrificing occupancy

Coastal Key Vacation Rentals · Florida Gulf Coast · 84 properties

The challenge

Coastal Key consistently achieved strong summer occupancy. Leadership initially viewed that as evidence that pricing was performing well. Our analysis showed another pattern. Several high-demand periods were reaching more than 70% occupancy four to six months before arrival while competing properties continued raising rates. The portfolio was selling its most valuable dates too quickly.

The strategy

  • Rebuilt competitive sets
  • Segmented beachfront and inland homes
  • Increased far-out premiums
  • Raised peak-event floors
  • Introduced stricter minimum stays during high-demand arrival periods
  • Reduced restrictions on weaker shoulder dates
+18.6%RevPAR
+14.2%ADR
+1.9 ptsOccupancy

The objective was not to maximize rate. It was to delay the sale of valuable inventory while demand was still building.

Case study 02

Recovering occupancy without blanket discounting

UrbanNest Stays · London · 46 serviced apartments

The challenge

UrbanNest entered the first quarter with forward occupancy materially behind the previous year. The initial instinct was to apply a portfolio-wide discount. Instead, we separated the problem by booking window and property segment. The largest weakness existed in Sunday-through-Thursday stays booked inside 21 days. Weekend demand remained healthy.

The strategy

  • Protected weekend pricing
  • Introduced more aggressive weekday last-minute adjustments
  • Reduced selected minimum stays
  • Created targeted mobile and advance-purchase promotions
  • Repositioned underperforming apartments against a more relevant competitive group
+15.1%RevPAR
+11.4%ADR
+3.3 ptsOccupancy

UrbanNest improved occupancy without turning every date into a discounted date.

Case study 03

Stopping long bookings from consuming premium holiday inventory

AlpineKey Retreats · Colorado and Utah · 127 luxury cabins and chalets

The challenge

Long-duration winter bookings appeared attractive. But several discounted stays were crossing Christmas, New Year and Presidents' Day dates that historically commanded substantially higher nightly rates. Average length of stay looked healthy. Revenue displacement did not.

The strategy

  • Introduced peak-period length-of-stay controls
  • Added maximum-stay rules around premium nights
  • Revised weekly discounts
  • Added event-specific pricing floors
  • Used more aggressive orphan-gap pricing after peak bookings arrived
+22.4%Peak-date ADR
StablePeak occupancy
+9.1%Revenue from difficult gaps

A long booking is not automatically a valuable booking.
