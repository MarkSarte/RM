REVENUE MANAGEMENT, NOT SET-AND-FORGET PRICING

PriceLabs can change rates. It still needs someone to decide what those rates should accomplish.

A pricing algorithm can process market signals, but it does not know whether your priority is protecting ADR, accelerating weekday occupancy, holding premium inventory, reducing stranded calendar gaps, or improving portfolio-level RevPAR.

That is where active revenue management begins: interpreting the signal, setting the guardrails, monitoring performance and intervening when the calendar behaves differently than expected.

THE FOUR REVENUE LEVERS

A simple framework for complex pricing decisions.

01

Price

Sell the right night at the right rate.

Base-price positioning, floors and ceilings, day-of-week demand, seasonality, local events, far-out premiums and last-minute adjustments.

02

Pace

Know when you are selling too fast—or too slowly.

Booking pickup and forward occupancy are reviewed against expected demand so pricing can move before weak performance becomes a last-minute problem.

03

Calendar

Make more of the inventory you already have.

Dynamic minimum stays, orphan-gap recovery and length-of-stay controls help reduce stranded inventory without discounting every open night.

04

Market

Price against the competitors that actually matter.

Relevant comp sets, market pickup and local demand are used to understand whether each listing is underpriced, overpriced or appropriately positioned.

THE DIAGNOSIS COMES FIRST

Sometimes the problem isn’t the price.

An empty night does not automatically mean the rate should be reduced. A restriction, booking-window mismatch or weak calendar pattern may be suppressing demand.

A rigid 3-night rule can block demand for a 2-night gap. The better action may be to open the stay rule—not cut the rate.

HOW ACTIVE MANAGEMENT WORKS

A disciplined rhythm behind every pricing decision.

01

Audit

Review PriceLabs settings, ADR, occupancy, RevPAR, booking pace, stay rules, comp positioning and upcoming demand.

02

Build

Define market position, rate guardrails, seasonal logic, occupancy rules, far-out strategy and last-minute behavior.

03

Monitor

Watch pickup, forward occupancy, events, high-demand dates, floor hits, gaps and other performance exceptions.

04

Intervene

Adjust the control that is actually causing the issue—price, stay rule, market position or booking-window strategy.

05

Communicate what happened, why it happened and what changes are being made next.

NO BLACK-BOX REVENUE MANAGEMENT

Clear decisions. Clear reasons. Clear next actions.

What happened?

Midweek forward occupancy moved behind target pace.

Why?

Market pickup softened and the current minimum stay is restricting short demand.

What next?

Open targeted two-night windows while preserving weekend rate integrity.
